From textile requirement to yarn specification

Begin with the fabric or product being developed, the role of the decorative yarn and the required metallic appearance. Metallic Yarn and Zari Yarn provide different product routes, while the end use helps narrow the discussion.

Share denier, TPM, twist, construction, colour or finish when those details are already known. Zari Lab explains the terminology so technical and purchasing teams can work from the same brief.

What production teams should share

Describe the intended textile, required appearance, yarn position or function, known specifications, quantity context and any existing sample.

Choose the product route

Compare product families before deciding whether the requirement begins with metallic yarn, zari yarn or a custom development discussion.

Supporting repeat manufacturing requirements

Use an approved sample or clearly identified specification as the reference for later production conversations. Keeping the product type, construction, colour or finish and application together makes repeat requirements easier to communicate.

Manufacturing outlines the pathway from specification review and sampling to production coordination, while Quality Control explains review against the agreed buyer brief. Buyer-specific development can begin through Custom Metallic Yarn.

Sample before production review

Use samples to evaluate the proposed product, construction and appearance before moving into the commercial requirement.

Prepare the repeat brief

Carry the approved reference, application, known technical details and quantity into the next RFQ.

What information helps a textile manufacturer start a yarn review?

Share the fabric or product, intended application, required metallic effect, colour or finish, known denier, TPM, twist or construction, and any sample reference available.

How should a repeat requirement be identified?

Reference the previously approved sample or specification and include any current application, quantity or construction details so the requirement can be reviewed in context.
